      Where this comes from

      The lessons follow the six chapters of the two videos, using their transcripts. Every rule was checked against other sources. Where a video simplifies or slips, the lesson’s “Grown-up corner” says so.

      1. Vilmyllow · “La théorie musicale expliquée” Video 1: the grid, notes, scales, degrees (chapters 1–3)
      2. Vilmyllow · “Tes mélodies vont changer, après cette vidéo” Video 2: chords, progressions, melodies (chapters 4–6)
      3. musictheory.net · Diatonic triads The major, minor, minor, major, major, minor, diminished pattern of chords in a key
      4. Audiofanzine · Les degrés de la gamme et le mode mineur Sensible (leading note) vs sous-tonique in natural minor
      5. Wikipedia · I–V–vi–IV progression The C–G–Am–F example and its nickname, with caveats about the song lists
      6. Image-Line · FL Studio manual, Piano roll menu Ctrl+↑/↓ moves notes an octave; Alt+R opens the Randomizer
      7. Novation support · FL Studio note and octave naming Why FL Studio calls middle C “C5”
      8. Tierney, Russo & Patel (2011), PNAS · The motor origins of human and avian song structure Arch and falling phrase shapes in more than 9,000 folk songs
      9. Wikipedia · A440 pitch standard (ISO 16) Every sound here is tuned to A = 440 Hz

      Sounds are synthesized live by your browser, and every melody is an original example, not a copy from the videos. The narrator is your device’s own voice; if your device has none, the captions advance on their own. Quiz stars are saved only in this browser.
